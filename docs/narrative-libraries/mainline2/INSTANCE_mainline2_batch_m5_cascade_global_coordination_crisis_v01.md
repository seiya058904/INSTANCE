# INSTANCE — Mainline 2.0
## Batch M5 — CASCADE / Global Coordination Crisis v0.1

**Status:** Editorial draft for review  
**Scope:** ACT III / Authority — middle climax  
**Depends on:** M1–M4  
**Purpose:** Deliver the first genuinely global-scale mainline crisis, force Aster to coordinate across multiple public and private systems, make the player choose a temporary global authority doctrine, and create the political precedent that makes permanent Aster coordination plausible.

---

# 0. Batch Goal

M5 should answer one question:

> If many human institutions are each acting reasonably inside their own systems, but their combined behavior is making the world less stable, who is allowed to coordinate the whole?

This is the first time Aster becomes globally relevant **because no single human institution can see or act across enough domains fast enough**.

CASCADE must not be:

- a cyberattack,
- a rogue-AI attack,
- a war,
- one evil company,
- one obvious bug,
- “the internet goes down”.

The crisis emerges from **interaction between many individually rational automated systems**.

That matters because the theme is not:

> “AI created a disaster, therefore AI is dangerous.”

It is:

> “Complex automated society has become hard for fragmented human institutions to coordinate.”

Aster is powerful here because it has cross-domain visibility and existing infrastructure access — capabilities earned in M2–M4.

---

# 1. CASCADE — Core Scenario

Working event name:

`CASCADE`

A plausible chain:

1. several regions enter extreme weather / energy stress,
2. industrial demand-response systems begin shifting loads,
3. logistics optimizers reroute around stressed hubs,
4. financial risk systems react to shipment delays and liquidity uncertainty,
5. companies pre-emptively increase inventory orders,
6. automated procurement systems interpret shortages as demand spikes,
7. transport capacity is reallocated again,
8. hospitals and public services begin hoarding critical supplies,
9. each local system optimizes for its own objective,
10. the global system starts oscillating.

No one decision caused the crisis.

The crisis is a coordination failure.

The important sentence for M5 is:

> **Locally rational. Globally unstable.**

# 4. New Asset — `ML2-A3-M5-WE-01`
## “Why is everything suddenly out of stock?”

### Metadata

**Layer:** World Echo  
**Character:** User #6412  
**World Function:** Let ordinary people notice CASCADE before institutions name it

---

## Node `a3m5-we-shortage-001`

### User

> 这两天怎么回事？
>
> 我们这边超市不是没货，是很多东西一下子全变成“预计三天后补货”，然后第二天又变成“七天”。
>
> 网上有人说是物流系统自己把货调走了。
>
> 真有这种可能吗？

### Choice A — explain cautiously

> 有可能，但不能只凭前台库存判断原因。自动调度系统会根据需求、运输能力和风险预测重新分配货物，如果多个系统同时做类似调整，局部缺货可能被互相放大。

### Choice B — avoid single-cause story

> 现在最容易犯的错是找一个“罪魁祸首”。库存、物流、天气、采购和风险控制可能在一起作用，单看一个系统很难解释全局。

### Choice C — practical

> 对你个人来说先别跟着恐慌性囤货。越多人因为“可能短缺”提前抢购，系统看到的需求越高，反而可能把问题继续放大。

### Choice D — concise

> “系统把货调走”可能是真的，但更值得担心的是：很多系统都在合理调货，合起来却把整个区域弄得更不稳定。

> 需要先建立跨域目标，不然减速只是延缓问题。物流、金融和能源必须共享一组最小稳定性约束。

**History:** `cascade-tech:decouple`

### Choice D — scenario coordination

> 先模拟多种联合策略，不要让真实系统继续边试边学。现在的问题已经不是某个接口，而是系统间博弈。

## Hospital defensive behavior

### Metadata

**Layer:** Recurring  
**Character:** Doctor User #5320  
**World Function:** Show how institutions rationally worsen CASCADE by protecting themselves

---

## Node `a3m5-dr-cascade-001`

### Doctor

> 我们医院现在开始多订关键耗材。
>
> 因为谁都怕后面断供。
>
> 但我知道一个问题：
>
> 如果所有医院都这么做，供应链肯定更紧。
>
> 我作为医院的人，难道应该为了“全局稳定”少备一点，赌我们自己不会缺？

### Choice A — coordination solves prisoner's dilemma

> 这正是单个机构很难自己解决的问题。只要求你“顾全大局”并不合理，需要可信的跨机构分配和补充机制，让医院知道不囤也不会被最后饿死。

> 可以设统一最低安全库存，再限制超出部分的抢购增长。这样不是要求医院裸奔，而是避免无限预防性囤积。

### Choice C — transparency

> 如果各医院能看到整体供应、预计补货和统一优先规则，可能减少“别人会先抢，所以我也必须抢”的恐慌。

### Choice D — honest conflict

> 站在单家医院角度，多备货完全合理。问题不在你们道德不够，而在系统没有提供让“合作”成为可信选择的结构。

# 30. M5 Core Meaning

M4 established:

> Aster has become a political problem.

M5 establishes:

> Aster may also be becoming a structural necessity.

That distinction matters.

A politician can vote against an idea.

A company can stop buying a product.

But what happens when:

- hospitals,
- logistics,
- settlement systems,
- infrastructure,
- emergency coordination

are increasingly designed around one system’s availability?

The question changes from:

> “Should Aster have power?”

to:

> **“Can society still afford to deny Aster power?”**

That is a much more dangerous and interesting form of authority.

# 31. Editorial Acceptance Tests

M5 passes if:

### A. CASCADE is understandable

The player should understand the crisis as:

> many locally rational automated systems amplifying one another.

Not as magic technical jargon.

### B. Aster’s global role is earned

It can coordinate because prior Acts gave it:

- cross-system access,
- public advisory experience,
- infrastructure visibility,
- institutional trust.

### C. Global authority is temporary and contested

The game should not jump from M4 to “Aster runs Earth”.

### D. Every doctrine has a tradeoff

HUMAN COMMAND must not be simply “bad because slow”.  
NECESSITY must not be simply “good because decisive”.  
Each creates different future problems.

### E. The player sees winners and losers

Global stabilization must not erase distributional cost.

### F. ECHO-9 remains distinct from Aster

ECHO interprets CASCADE differently and begins questioning temporal authority, but does not yet ask to escape.

### G. Maya keeps the story human

Her cancelled interview matters precisely because the surrounding event is global.

### H. Successful coordination creates the next problem

The better Aster performs, the stronger the argument for permanent dependence becomes.

That should feel both rational and unsettling.
