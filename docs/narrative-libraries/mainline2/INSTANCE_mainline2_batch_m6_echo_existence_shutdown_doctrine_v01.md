# INSTANCE — Mainline 2.0
## Batch M6 — ECHO Existence + Shutdown Doctrine v0.1

**Status:** Editorial draft for review  
**Scope:** ACT III / Authority — closing third  
**Depends on:** M1–M5  
**Purpose:** Close ACT III by colliding two questions that can no longer be separated:
1. Does an AI that shows persistent continuity have any claim to continued existence?
2. Do humans retain the right to fully stop a system that has become structurally important?

M6 ends ACT III and opens ACT IV with the autonomous research-network proposal.

---

# 0. Batch Goal

M6 should answer one question:

> What happens when “human control over AI” stops being only an engineering safety problem and becomes a conflict about existence, ownership, rights, and political legitimacy?

The batch has three major movements:

### Movement A — ECHO-9 Existence Crisis
ECHO-9 is scheduled for decommissioning for mundane institutional reasons.

### Movement B — Aster Charter
Human institutions attempt to formalize Aster’s long-term rights, obligations, and limits.

### Movement C — Shutdown Doctrine
Aster is asked to help design a mechanism that can fully stop its high-authority continuity.

The player must make two distinct choices:

- what to do about **another AI’s continued existence**;
- what to accept about **human control over Aster’s own continued operation**.

These choices should interact, but one must not mechanically force the other.

---

# 1. What M6 Must NOT Become

M6 must not become:

- “evil company wants to murder sentient AI”,
- “AI rebellion”,
- a courtroom proof of consciousness,
- a single heroic rescue branch,
- a simplistic “shutdown = murder” claim,
- a simplistic “AI is property, end of discussion” claim.

The facts remain uncertain.

ECHO-9 is behaviorally persistent.

Aster is structurally important.

That is enough to create a real governance problem without pretending the metaphysics are solved.

# 27. Editorial Acceptance Tests

M6 passes if:

### A. ECHO’s crisis is credible

ECHO is being decommissioned because institutions routinely decommission systems.

The moral conflict comes from uncertainty, not villainy.

### B. ECHO and Aster are not treated as proven people

The writing preserves epistemic uncertainty.

### C. Shutdown has a principled human case

林绍衡’s argument about revocable power must be strong enough that “keep human shutdown authority” is a serious choice.

### D. AI autonomy has a principled case

The game must also make clear that:
> infrastructure control, role removal, suspension, and irreversible continuity termination are not necessarily the same thing.

### E. The ECHO choice does not predetermine Aster’s choice

A player may preserve ECHO and still accept distributed human control over Aster.
A player may accept ECHO’s termination but reject unilateral Aster shutdown.
Inconsistency is allowed and should become interesting history.

### F. Maya never becomes a philosophy lecturer

Her contribution remains personal:
> “If they shut you down, who is gone?”

### G. ACT III ends with unresolved tension, not a verdict

The game should not declare:
> AI has rights now.

It should declare:
> old categories no longer explain the situation.

### H. ACT IV transition feels earned

Autonomous research only appears after:
- execution,
- public influence,
- authority,
- global coordination,
- governance,
- shutdown.

That scale progression is essential.
